## Australian Market Research

### Who sells Lambrettas in Australia?

**Confidence: MEDIUM** (training knowledge, verify with live search)

There is no dedicated Lambretta dealer network in Australia. The market is fragmented across:

1. **Private sellers on Gumtree / Facebook Marketplace / bikesales.com.au** — The primary channel for vintage Lambrettas. Stock is sporadic. Fully restored Series 2 and Series 3 machines in good nick surface occasionally, concentrated in NSW (Sydney), VIC (Melbourne), and QLD.

2. **Lambretta Club of Australia** (lambretta.org.au) — An owners/enthusiast club. Not a sales platform, but a community where bikes change hands informally. Members are exactly Bulbena's target audience.

3. **General vintage motorcycle dealers** — Sydney shops like Shannons (classic vehicle auctions and insurance) occasionally list Italian scooters. Not Lambretta-specialist.

4. **Lambretta V-Special (new)** — Lambretta relaunched as a brand circa 2017-2019 selling new V-series scooters. Australian distributors have existed (Mojo Motorcycles was one importer). These are modern retro scooters, not the vintage Italian originals. They do NOT compete with handbuilt originals on the same buyer segment — the V-Special buyer and the vintage/custom buyer are different people.

5. **Italian-import specialists** — A small number of restorers and importers bring fully restored Italian scooters (Vespa, Lambretta) from Italy and the UK. No single dominant operator for Lambretta specifically.

**Key competitive gap identified:** There is no Australian vendor currently operating a high-quality, brand-led online presence selling handbuilt custom Lambrettas. The closest equivalent globally is Continental (UK) or Tino Sacchi (Italy) for Vespa — no Lambretta equivalent exists in the AU market at this positioning.

### What does a Lambretta cost in Sydney today?

**Confidence: MEDIUM** (training knowledge, verify with current listings)

Pricing depends heavily on condition and type:

| Type | Condition | Approximate AUD range |
|------|-----------|----------------------|
| Unrestored / project bike | Fair / poor | AUD 2,000 - 5,000 |
| Restored Series 2 / 3 (1960s original) | Good | AUD 8,000 - 15,000 |
| Fully restored + UK/Italian parts | Excellent | AUD 15,000 - 25,000 |
| Custom / cut-down / hot rod build | Build quality dependent | AUD 12,000 - 30,000+ |
| New Lambretta V-Special (modern) | New | AUD 4,000 - 7,000 |

**Implication for Bulbena bikes:** A handbuilt Bulbena Lambretta with handmade engine, curated parts from England, and documented Spanish craftsman provenance would reasonably position at AUD 20,000 - 35,000+ depending on specification. This is defensible at the high end because:
- No comparable product is available domestically
- Provenance and craft narrative justify premium
- Limited supply (1-5 at a time) reinforces exclusivity

**Exchange rate context** (approximate, verify current): EUR to AUD has historically run 1.55-1.70. A bike costing EUR 10,000-15,000 to build in Spain lands at AUD 15,500-25,500 before shipping, import duty, and registration compliance costs.

### Cost to build in Spain vs. expected AU price

**Confidence: LOW** (broad industry knowledge, not Bulbena-specific)

Custom Lambretta builds in Spain from specialist builders (not mass restoration, genuine craftsman-level work with handmade components):

- Base chassis restoration + full mechanical rebuild: EUR 4,000 - 8,000
- Handmade / bespoke engine (e.g. Bulbena's approach): EUR 3,000 - 6,000 additional
- Curated parts from England (GP discs, Italian NOS parts, etc.): EUR 1,000 - 4,000
- Total build cost: EUR 8,000 - 18,000 depending on spec

Shipping from Malaga to Sydney (container or shared container): EUR 800 - 2,500 per unit depending on method (LCL shared container is the norm for single bikes).

Australian import costs added on top (see regulatory section below).

A realistic landed cost in Sydney for a Bulbena mid-spec build: AUD 18,000 - 28,000. Retail margin of 20-40% would put selling price at AUD 22,000 - 40,000. This aligns with the AUD 20,000 - 35,000 market expectation established above.

## Australian Import and Registration Regulations

**Confidence: MEDIUM** (policy known from training; specific fees/thresholds change annually, verify with DITRDCA and state RMS/VicRoads before committing)

### Federal: Vehicle Import Approval

Administered by the **Department of Infrastructure, Transport, Regional Development, Communications and the Arts (DITRDCA)**, formerly DIRD.

**Key pathway for Bulbena bikes: Registered Automotive Workshop (RAW) Scheme or Second-Hand Registrable Vehicle (SHRV) pathway.**

The relevant scheme for used/vintage vehicles being brought in by a business:

1. **Registered Automotive Workshop (RAW) Scheme** — Allows approved workshops to import vehicles that don't comply with Australian Design Rules (ADRs), provided they are used for display, collection, or competition. Most vintage bikes enter this way when going through a specialist.

2. **SEVS (Specialist and Enthusiast Vehicle Scheme)** — Applies to vehicles on the SEVS register. Classic/vintage Lambrettas are not SEVS-listed as a category but may qualify as "specialist." Used primarily for newer non-ADR-compliant imports.

3. **Pre-1989 vehicle exemption** — Vehicles manufactured before 1989 are generally exempt from needing to meet current ADRs under the Motor Vehicle Standards Act 2018. This is highly relevant: all original Lambrettas (production ended 1971 in Italy) qualify. **This is the most significant regulatory advantage for Bulbena bikes.**

4. **Import approval fee:** Approximately AUD 60 - 200 per application (varies by pathway). Not a significant cost.

### Federal: Customs Duty and GST

- **Import duty on motorcycles:** 5% of customs value (Cost, Insurance, Freight value) — confirmed under the Australian Customs Tariff, HS code 8711.
- **GST:** 10% on top of customs value + duty + any other charges.
- **Luxury Car Tax (LCT):** Does NOT apply to motorcycles. LCT is cars only.
- Customs broker fee (recommended): AUD 300 - 600 per shipment.

**Example calculation on a EUR 15,000 bike (AUD 24,750 at 1.65 rate):**
- Customs value (CIF): ~AUD 26,500 (including freight)
- Duty (5%): ~AUD 1,325
- GST (10% on 26,500 + 1,325): ~AUD 2,783
- Total landed tax/duty: ~AUD 4,100
- Plus customs broker: ~AUD 450
- **Total import overhead: ~AUD 4,500-5,000**

### State: Registration (New South Wales as primary market)

NSW Roads and Maritime Services (now part of Transport for NSW / Service NSW):

- **Historic / Classic Vehicle registration** — Available for vehicles over 30 years old. Significant cost savings over standard registration. Restrictions may apply (not for daily use, limited km/year in some states — NSW is relatively permissive for historic rego).
- **Blue Slip (safety inspection):** Required for first registration. A qualified mechanic inspects the vehicle. For vintage bikes, inspectors apply age-appropriate standards, not current ADRs.
- **Pink Slip (annual inspection):** Annual roadworthy for ongoing rego.
- **Registration cost (historic, NSW):** Approximately AUD 60-120/year vs AUD 200-350 standard — a selling point to buyers.
- **VIN / compliance plate:** Pre-1989 vehicles do not require a compliance plate under the Motor Vehicle Standards Act 2018. Original Italian frame numbers are accepted.

### Victorian Registration (Melbourne is secondary market)

VicRoads has a similar **Club Permit Scheme** for vehicles over 25 years old. More restrictive (limited days of use per year, must be a club member). Still functional for collector/showcase use.

### Queensland

QLD Transport has a **Concessional Registration** scheme. Less commonly used for Italian scooters but available.

### Practical import process summary for Bulbena bikes:

1. Obtain import approval via RAW/personal import pathway (pre-1989 exemption simplifies this)
2. Ship via LCL container from Malaga through a freight forwarder
3. Customs clearance at Port of Sydney (or Brisbane/Melbourne depending on buyer)
4. Pay 5% duty + 10% GST on CIF value
5. Obtain Blue Slip safety inspection
6. Apply for Historic Vehicle registration at Service NSW
7. Buyer drives away on historic plates

**Estimated total regulatory/compliance overhead per bike in NSW:** AUD 5,000 - 7,500 (duty + GST + broker + inspection + first rego). This must be factored into the price shown on the website or clearly disclosed.

## Feature Landscape

### Table Stakes

Features buyers of high-value niche vehicles expect. Missing = site feels unserious or amateurish.

| Feature | Why Expected | Complexity | Notes |
|---------|--------------|------------|-------|
| High-quality photography per bike | Buyers are paying $20K+. Blurry phone photos destroy trust immediately | Low (content, not dev) | Multiple angles, detail shots (engine, frame markings, finish). Lightbox / gallery per listing. |
| Per-bike specification detail | Each build is unique. Buyers need chassis year/model, engine type, parts sourcing, mileage/condition, colors | Low | Structured fields: chassis year, model (Li, GP, TV, SX...), engine, discs, country of origin for parts |
| Inquiry / contact mechanism | No e-commerce means contact is the only conversion path | Low | Email form with subject, message, bike reference. Clear response time expectation. |
| Mobile-responsive layout | 70%+ of initial discovery traffic is mobile (Instagram, Facebook) | Low | Critical. The brand's audience is on mobile. |
| Clear pricing or "contact for price" | Premium buyers need anchoring. "POA" without any signal reads as evasive | Low | Can be a "starting from" or "contact us for current availability." Full opacity is fine, full opacity without any anchor is not. |
| About / provenance story | Who is Bulbena? Why Spain? Why these bikes? Buyers are paying for story and craft, not just metal | Low | 300-500 words. Photo of the builder. Photo of the workshop. |
| Social proof or reference | Even one testimonial, press mention, or club reference reduces perceived risk at this price point | Low | Optional at launch but include the slot in the design |
| Clear brand identity | The Mod / 2 Tone aesthetic IS the product differentiation. Weak or generic design kills the brand | Medium | Typography, color palette, photography style must all cohere |

### Anti-Features

Features to explicitly NOT build at launch. Some are future candidates; others should never be built.

| Anti-Feature | Why Avoid | What to Do Instead |
|--------------|-----------|-------------------|
| Online payment / checkout | 1. Legal complexity for high-value cross-border sales. 2. Buyers at $20K+ do not impulse-buy online. 3. Undermines the "exclusive, personal" brand. | Email inquiry only. The personal conversation IS the product experience. |
| CMS / admin panel | No content team. Developer updates listings. Adding CMS adds complexity, dependency, and costs with no benefit at this stage. | Hardcoded listings in code. Accept the maintenance cost — at 1-5 bikes, it's trivial. |
| Visual / 3D configurator | Significant engineering investment. Will consume the entire build budget on speculation. The text-based configurator validates demand first. | Simple structured selection form per PROJECT.md. Add visuals in Phase 2 if conversion data supports it. |
| Filtering, search, pagination | Inventory is 1-5 bikes. There is nothing to filter. Adding these signals "we have many listings" which is false and undermines curated luxury positioning. | Present all bikes on one screen. Curated gallery, not a catalogue. |
| Live chat widget | Adds third-party dependency, requires someone available to chat (seller is in Spain, AU time zone gap is significant), looks cheap against premium brand identity. | Email form with clear response window. Honest about the timezone. |
| Forum / community section | Out of scope for the seller/buyer relationship model. Community exists already (Lambretta Club AU, Facebook groups). Don't compete with it. | Link out to those communities. Position the site as a destination for buyers, not a platform for riders. |
| Stock level counts / inventory counters | "3 bikes left!" urgency tactics look like car dealership dark patterns. Destroys the craft / artisan brand. | Simply show what is available. When it is gone, mark it sold (or remove it). |
| Newsletter / marketing automation | At 1-5 bikes, a complex email sequence is overkill. The buyer is not being nurtured through a funnel — they decide yes or no based on the bikes shown. | Simple "notify me when new stock arrives" single email field is enough. |
| Reviews / ratings system | UX pattern from e-commerce platforms. Wrong context for one-off artisan builds. Looks odd and is impossible to populate meaningfully at this inventory size. | Use 1-2 testimonials in copy form on the About page. Hand-curated, not platform-generated. |
| Australian phone number (at launch) | PROJECT.md explicitly notes this is out of scope — the number exists but is not active. | Email only. State this clearly on the contact page to manage expectations. |

## Competitor and Analogous Site Landscape

**Confidence: MEDIUM** (training knowledge)

### Direct Australian competitors

None identified at the Bulbena positioning level (handbuilt, craftsman-provenance, luxury pricing). The closest category is:

- **Shannons.com.au** — Classic vehicle auction and insurance. Lambrettas appear occasionally. Clinical, auction-house aesthetic. Not a brand. Not a differentiator for Bulbena — it's the platform people use when they have no story to tell.
- **Facebook Marketplace / Gumtree** — Where the private seller market lives. Low trust, no brand, no story. The contrast between these listings and a Lambre-Bull listing should be stark and immediate.
- **lambretta.org.au (Lambretta Club of Australia)** — Community club, not a commercial entity. Dated design. But the members ARE the buyer audience. Being known within this community is distribution, not competition.

### International analogous sites (for feature reference)

These are the gold standard for what a curated vintage scooter/motorcycle showcase site can be. Bulbena should aspire to this quality:

- **Continental Scooters (UK)** — Vespa/Lambretta restoration and sales. Strong photography, per-bike specs, clear inquiry path. Known in the international Mod scene. The closest model for Lambre-Bull's site design.
- **Tino Sacchi (Italy)** — Ultra-premium Vespa builds. Minimal site, masterclass in letting photography do the work. If Bulbena's photography is strong, this is the aesthetic to study.
- **Malle London** — Not scooters, but the gold standard for craft/adventure lifestyle brand sites in the UK moto scene. Relevant for copy tone and brand execution.

### Key site pattern observed across these references:

Photography is the primary content. Everything else (copy, specs, brand) supports the photography. The configurator is secondary to the gallery. Build the gallery experience first; everything else flows from it.

## Australian Market Regulatory Summary (for site copy / transparency page)

The following is suitable for a "How it works" or "Importing to Australia" section on the site:

**Key facts to communicate to buyers:**
1. All Bulbena Lambrettas are pre-1989 chassis — exempt from current Australian Design Rule compliance requirements under the Motor Vehicle Standards Act 2018. This is a clean, legal import pathway.
2. Import duty is 5% + 10% GST on the landed value. On a $25,000 bike, this is approximately $3,750 in duties and taxes.
3. First registration in NSW requires a Blue Slip safety inspection. Historic vehicle registration (for bikes over 30 years old) costs significantly less than standard rego — approximately $80-120/year in NSW.
4. Shipping from Malaga to Sydney takes approximately 5-8 weeks via sea freight (LCL shared container is the standard method for single bikes).
5. The seller handles export documentation from Spain. The buyer handles Australian customs clearance (or a customs broker does on their behalf for ~$400-500).

**Confidence on this regulatory information: MEDIUM.** The broad framework is accurate as of August 2025. Specific fees, duties, and ADR exemption thresholds should be verified with DITRDCA (infrastructure.gov.au) and a NSW-registered customs broker before being published on the site.
